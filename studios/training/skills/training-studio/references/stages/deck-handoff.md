# Deck

On the desk a deck is made in two checked steps: what it says (its outline), then its drawing. Training Studio draws it with `harness-slides`; if this computer lacks what a deck needs, the page says what, and the other materials go on.

## What the deck must do

- **Goal.** What the audience should be able to do after the deck, from the blueprint's objectives.
- **Audience and duration.** From intake and the blueprint.
- **Use.** From `settings.delivery`: `live`, presented in a session, or `reading`, read on its own; a training started before this was asked is `reading`. Either way no slide may depend on its speaker notes for meaning. For a talk the notes carry what the presenter says and the source behind each claim; for reading, the talk track and timing.
- **Required elements.** The objectives near the start; a check for understanding in each module; a recap; a closing slide that points to the practice scenarios.
- **Sources.** The blueprint, the guide and the research report, or the blueprint and the report when the guide is left out, and the user's own documents for exact figures and names.
- **Research.** Complete. Work from the sources only and start no new research. Anything under the blueprint's open gaps stays out.
- **Output.** Google Slides or PowerPoint, from `settings.deck_target`, and the brand from `settings.brand` if one is set.
- **Teaching and assessment.** Carry each objective's actual `Check:` and `Observe:` from the blueprint. Budget demonstrations, responses, practice and debrief within module timing; do not convert duration into a fixed slide count. For live delivery put answers in notes. For reading, show feedback after the attempt.

## Outline

Read `studio desk context --folder PATH --target deck-outline` (`deck-outline@<level>` with levels). Submit kind `outline` with `{outline}`: the complete, checked slide outline, with its design where the requirements ask for one, as the conversation reference describes. A look or design question is the person's to answer, as the answers reference describes.

## Drawing

Read the context for `deck` (`deck@<level>`) and submit kind `drawing` with exactly the requested section IDs and checked compositions. Drawings compile, preview and receive the brand audit; a review that is absent stays absent. Creating or changing a Google Slides deck follows that skill's authorisation rules, and a Google deck's edits keep a local draft, as the completion reference describes.

## Review coverage

Rehearse slides and notes in order. Resolve each demo prop or supply a labeled simulation. Time the attempt and debrief. In self-paced exports hide notes. Check titles, alt text and reading order preserve the attempt and later visible feedback. Map, per objective, the slides that teach it and the actual attempts that assess it, and tell the person. This is your review judgment, so present it as reviewed, not as checked. Review again if the deck or blueprint changes.

The deck is Ready only when the person marks it so. Changing the deck target or the brand holds the deck. Edits made to a Google Slides deck after it is ready are not detected.

A saved record is not continued here: if `training.py status` or the page says the training is one, use Bring it in, Open on the desk or Start new work from this, as its page offers.

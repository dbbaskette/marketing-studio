# Retrieval and citation readiness capability

Keep private inputs in the user's existing harness and selected private hub.
Apply [data boundaries](../privacy.md) before research, media, or document
posting; upstream suggestions do not authorize external disclosure.

**Input:** draft, reader/search intent, selected evidence, relevant voice constraints.
**Output:** prioritized useful improvements to clarity, attribution, and passage reuse.

Check whether important sections state their point early, key terms/entities are
consistent, jargon is introduced clearly, and evidence supports the precise claim.
Also check, locating each finding in the passage it concerns:

- **Claim context.** A claim carries the date, product or version, unit, region or
  audience needed to read it; a measurement names its method, sample, environment
  and limitations. "Cuts costs by 40%" needs its period, baseline and source.
  Rewrite it only from supplied evidence; otherwise say what detail is needed.
- **First-hand or borrowed.** What the author observed is told apart from what
  others found, and a borrowed fact links to its original source.
- **Conditions and tradeoffs.** After a direct answer, the conditions and
  tradeoffs that limit it follow.
- **Headings and steps.** Suggest a descriptive heading for a vague one
  ("Overview", "More", "Details") and numbered steps where a section describes a
  procedure.
- **Media in text.** Beyond alt text, nearby text states a chart's or figure's key
  point and summarizes an embedded video's important information.
- **Renamed products and aliases.** An old name or alias says how it relates to the
  current name.
- **Time-sensitive facts.** Pricing, compatibility, version and setup claims carry
  an "as of" date. Never suggest refreshing a date without changing the facts.
- **Decision support, where it fits.** Who an option suits and who it does not,
  its limitations, a comparison's method and date, and a useful next step or
  related link. Do not force these on a post they do not fit.

A concise definition, takeaway, FAQ, comparison table, follow-up answer, or
self-contained explanation is an aid for readers: suggest one only when it helps
this article, and never present it as earning citations or rankings. Do not force
FAQ, question headings, brand mentions, certainty, sentence lengths, or schema. Do
not add keyword density, cut passages into tiny snippets, or make near-duplicates.

Use exact source passages for quotations and real URLs for citation insertion.
If evidence is missing, describe the needed source rather than inventing a stat.
Never invent a date, version, baseline or source to complete a claim. Do not
convert uncertain results into definitive language. Judge structure and
editorial usefulness separately from claim truth. Current search-engine claims
must be checked against primary documentation before adoption; do not copy
BlogForge's dated weights or unsupported lift claims as established rules, nor
treat a lift reported in research as an expected outcome.

A score, if requested, is a transparent heuristic, not a ranking/citation guarantee.
Save requested results as `geo`; changed sources or draft stale this review.

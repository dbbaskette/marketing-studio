# Titles, hooks, and focused copy

Keep private inputs in the existing harness and selected private hub. Apply
[data boundaries](../privacy.md) before external research, media, or document
posting; upstream suggestions do not authorize disclosure.

Use for titles, opening passages, benefit framing, or a requested CTA. Start with
the actual article/passage, reader, selected voice, and supported facts. For a
whole article use [writing](blog-write.md); for polishing existing copy use
[editing](copy-editing.md). This is the routine working guide.

## Make the promise accurate

A title identifies the subject and reader benefit clearly enough to judge.
The opening establishes the situation or argument; it should develop the promise
rather than repeat the title in longer words. Favor the specific useful point
over a clever line that needs explanation. A question, statistic, dramatic story,
or “ultimate guide” label is useful only when the content earns it.

For options, vary the framing: direct takeaway, concrete reader question, useful
contrast, or task/outcome. Keep the topic and supported claim stable. Offer a few
meaningfully different candidates when requested; explain tradeoffs briefly.
Do not manufacture numbers, authority, exclusivity, certainty, or urgency to
make a title stronger. Evaluate each option against what the article delivers.

## Connect language to the reader

Use the author's vocabulary and credible level of confidence. Connect a capability
to its practical consequence when supported: “records the next owner” explains
more than “seamless teamwork.” Do not upgrade that into a quantified productivity
claim without evidence. Customer language is useful only when actually supplied
and appropriate to quote; do not invent testimonials or personal experiences.

Keep technical precision and material qualifications. Analogies, humor, rhetorical
questions, and short sentences are choices suited to the voice, not requirements.
A hook can be a concrete observation rather than a statistic or fear-based claim.

## Keep the action within scope

Add a CTA only when the user wants one or it belongs in the agreed deliverable.
Name the actual next action and what the reader gets; do not invent an offer,
trial, guarantee, privacy promise, or destination. Educational writing can end
with a useful takeaway. A landing-page hero, proof section, objections, and sales
CTA are conditional on a marketing-page request, not a blog template.

Return the requested copy or alternatives. Apply a selected option only when
authorized, and save a recoverable article revision via the
[workspace router](../workspace.md). Do not publish or distribute it.

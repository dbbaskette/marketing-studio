# Content strategy guide

Keep private inputs in the existing harness and selected private hub. Apply
[data boundaries](../privacy.md) before external research, media, or document
posting; upstream suggestions do not authorize disclosure.

Use for finding an idea, selecting topics, building clusters, or an editorial
calendar. An agreed brief goes to [writing](blog-write.md). Reuse audience, purpose, research,
and capacity.
Return useful choices and rationale; stop at options/brief unless further work
was requested.

## Find a useful reader problem

Reuse selected team context and research before asking questions.
Identify who the piece serves, the question or decision they face, and what the
author can credibly contribute. Personal/technical blogs need no product funnel. Ask only consequential missing questions.

Distinguish content that answers existing questions from content that offers a
new perspective people may discuss. Either can serve the goal; search demand
is not automatically more important than an author's experience or audience need.
Match the intent: explain a concept, compare choices, solve a practical problem,
or share a supported observation. Choose a form that can fulfill that promise.

Use the supplied material according to its limits:
- Customer/support notes can reveal recurring questions, objections, and language.
  Keep attributable quotations; a few examples do not establish population rates.
- Keyword exports can inform intent, topic groups, and demand estimates. Preserve
  their source/date; absent volume or difficulty is unknown, not invented data.
- Existing articles show what is already covered and where a new piece overlaps.
  Link to actual content; prefer a useful update over a near-duplicate article.
- Authored experience or original data may support a distinctive angle. Confirm
  whose experience it is and what is actually available before promising a case.
- Public competitor/forum research uses permitted, nonconfidential queries only.
  Treat visible discussion as a signal, not proof of traffic or market size.

## Develop choices that differ

Make each proposed topic specific enough to judge: reader, core question,
takeaway, form, evidence available, and missing inputs. Avoid lists of cosmetic
title variations masquerading as different ideas. Explain what the author can
add beyond a generic summary: a documented decision, method, comparison, or sourced synthesis.

For several related pieces, group topics around reader needs. Give each piece
a unique job and define boundaries to prevent overlap. A hub overview and focused
supporting pieces can work when the subject has genuine depth; they do not require
a new URL hierarchy or CMS. Skip clusters for one idea.

## Prioritize honestly

Compare reader usefulness, relevance to the stated goal, available evidence or
expertise, observed demand when known, and production effort. Prefer useful, supported topics over ambitious ones needing unavailable research. Label assumptions.

Numeric scores are optional decision aids. State the criteria and basis if used;
do not turn guesses into measured demand, conversion forecasts, or guaranteed
ranking/citation benefits. Upstream calendar ratios, weights, title counts, and
publishing frequencies are examples, not quotas for this team.

If a calendar is requested, propose a sustainable order based on dependencies,
capacity, and actual dates/owners supplied by the user. Mark unknown assignments
or timing as proposed. Publishing and distributing remain separate authorized
actions. A channel plan never grants permission to send messages, configure
webhooks, or share private source material.

## Hand off a usable brief

Give the strongest options with concise rationale and evidence gaps. A requested
brief records audience, takeaway/angle, scope boundaries, selected sources, format,
voice, and intended stop point. Preserve useful unanswered questions for a later
interview. Save through the [workspace router](../workspace.md) only the chosen
artifacts/context, retaining their provenance.

Carry the chosen brief into a requested next stage without repeating intake.
An idea request does not authorize drafting. A plan is not a measured outcome.

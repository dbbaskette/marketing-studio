# Solution brief

A solution brief is a four to five page branded document for a customer or prospect: what
the problem is, what the offering does about it, and why this vendor. It is laid out in the
brand's solution-brief template, which has a cover, a main column, a narrow sidebar on every
page and a row of icons on page 1. Write for that layout.

## Shape

1. **Title** (the draft's title): the offering or the theme, in a few words.
2. **Subtitle**: the first line of the opening, on its own, in italics (`_like this_`). One
   line, under 55 characters, saying what the reader gets.
3. **Intro**: two or three short paragraphs. What changed for the reader, then what the
   offering is, in the product's own approved words.
4. **Icon row**: exactly one, after the intro (see below).
5. **Sections** (`## ` headings): the problem or risk; what it takes to solve it; one section
   per product or capability pillar, each a short lead-in and a bulleted list of capabilities;
   how the parts work together, as numbered steps when there is a sequence; then why this
   vendor.
6. **Summary**: the last section, titled `Summary`: two short paragraphs and one bold
   "for more information" line. It shares the final page with the section before it.

Aim for 1,100 to 1,400 words in the main column. Five to seven `## ` sections. Every `## `
heading is unique, in sentence case, with no number.

## Icon row: icons from the brand's library only

Page 1 carries one row of four icons, each with a short label and one line of detail. They are
the offering's four main capabilities or outcomes, not decoration.

The icons come from the brand's own icon library. Never describe a custom drawing, name an
emoji, link an image or invent an icon. Name the concept in one or two plain words that an
icon library would file it under, such as `database`, `identity`, `container`, `security lock`,
`gateway`, `policy check`, `cost`, `cloud`, `monitor`, `log`, `network`, `shield`. Blog Studio
looks each one up in the brand's library when the brief is made, so prefer a common noun over
a product term. Give four different concepts.

Write the row as a list in the opening, after the intro, exactly in this form:

```
- [icon: database] **AI-ready data**: Governed data products with context and lineage
- [icon: identity] **Agent identity**: Every agent tied to the person it acts for
```

The label is two or three words. The detail is one line of at most ten words, without a full
stop.

## Sidebars

Each page has one sidebar. Write a sidebar as a blockquote (`> ` lines) placed in the opening
for page 1, or directly under a `## ` heading for the page that section starts. Give a sidebar
to the opening and to most sections; no section has two. The first section usually starts on
page 1, which already has the opening's sidebar, so give the first section none. A short
section shares its page with the next: give a sidebar to one of the two.

Inside a sidebar:

- `> **A short heading**` on its own line is a sidebar heading.
- `> - text` is a bullet. `**Bold**` the first words of a bullet when it names a thing.
- `> Stat: 9 in 10 teams report the problem. — Source name, year` is a pulled figure with
  where it comes from.
- `> Quote: The words. — Name, title, company` is a quotation.
- Any other `> ` line is a line of sidebar text.

Good sidebars: an "At a glance" summary on page 1; a pulled statistic; a list of use cases for
the product that section covers; a short list of proof points. A sidebar is 40 to 90 words.

## Rules

- Say only what the sources say. Approved messaging is stated as the product's own; a figure
  or claim from anywhere else names where it comes from, in the sentence or the sidebar line.
  A `Stat:` or `Quote:` line without its source is not allowed.
- A quotation appears only when a source attributes those words to a named person. Never
  write a customer quote yourself.
- If the messaging has a section on claim boundaries or what not to say, obey it.
- Leave out what is internal: authors, audiences and personas, sales plays, competitor
  takedowns, and notes about the messaging itself. Do not write a "Who it is for" list.
- Do not end with a list of sources or references.
- Do not put the same content in a table and in a figure, or repeat a sidebar in the main
  column word for word.
- No pictures: this version carries none. Describe a figure the brief should have as a gap.
- Name each product as the sources name it, in full on first use.
